# Planet Ongaku — Ordinary Life & Civilian Institutions

> **Status:** Proposal for creator review. Nothing here is canon yet.
> Companion to [`PLANET-ONGAKU-WORLD-BIBLE.md`](PLANET-ONGAKU-WORLD-BIBLE.md) §3 and §11.

This is the layer that stops Planet Ongaku feeling like a game map and starts making it somewhere
people live. When the Sick 52 attack something, we should be watching it happen to people whose
ordinary lives we have already seen.

---

## Part 1 — The Civilian Column

**The rule:** every named character in the universe gets a *civilian column* — even fighters, even
bosses, even mutants where it applies. If a character has no answer to these six fields, they are
not a character yet.

| Field | Question |
| --- | --- |
| **Work** | What do they do on a Tuesday? |
| **Home** | Which district, and who else lives there? |
| **People** | Partner, children, parents, best friend, the person they owe |
| **Routine** | The thing they do every week without fail |
| **Vice** | What they spend money on that they should not |
| **Secret** | The thing that would cost them everything |

### Worked examples

| Character | Work | Home | People | Routine | Vice | Secret |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Marcus "Grand Verse" Cole** (A♠) | Chairman; publisher on paper | The Heights, alone since his wife died | Sister Delores (Q♠), cousin June (9♠) | **Golf, Sunday, 7am, same four men, 22 years** | Rare vinyl at absurd prices | He has never told anyone he cannot read music |
| **Amara "Freq Kid" Nwosu** (Signed, 19) | Recording artist under contract | Southside, with her grandmother | Grandmother; a producer she should not trust | Writes on the front steps every night | Spends advances on her family, not herself | She has already been offered the 10♠ seat |
| **Joss Karume** (NexaGen analyst) | Mid-level analyst, NexaGen Sound | Central District apartment | Dating someone from a rival organisation | Thursday five-a-side football | Buys clothes he cannot afford | Who he is dating |
| **Nadia "Silk" Silk** (A♥) | Runs the Velvet Static venue group | Rose Hill, penthouse | Estranged from her mother in Southside | Swims at 5am before anyone is awake | Never drinks — control is the vice | She funds a music school in her old neighbourhood anonymously |
| **Lt. Sana "Rook" Delacroix** (Aero Command) | Interceptor pilot | Base housing near Skyport | Younger brother at Ongaku University | Calls her brother every Sunday | Illegal street racing on leave — with Chrome's crew | Her commanding officer knows |
| **Kenneth "Blackout" Sayles** (2♠) | Enforcer; gym owner | Southside, above the gym | Wife, two daughters | Church, every Sunday, front row | None. That is the problem | He has decided he is leaving the life |

> Note what these examples do: **Rook races with Chrome's crew**, which quietly connects Aero Command
> to the Hip Hop Mafia through a hobby rather than a plot. That is how the ordinary-life layer earns
> its keep — connections that already exist before a story needs them.

## Part 2 — Restaurants & food

The most important category, because it is where people **meet**. Once a place is named, keep it.

### The important rooms

| Name | What it is | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Vantaggio's** | 60-year-old steakhouse, Old Quarter. White tablecloths, no music, waiters who have been there decades | **Where the Commission meets** (first Sunday of the month, back room). The single most important address in the criminal world |
| **The Velvet Room** | Members-only supper club owned by Velvet Records, a room in every major city | Where R&B stories happen — deals, affairs, reconciliations staged for the cameras |
| **Ma Kade's** | Soul food. Started as one Southside kitchen, now **40 locations** | Owned through the Kade Community Trust. Genuinely feeds the neighbourhood **and** genuinely launders money. The whole faction's ambiguity in one restaurant |
| **Skillet & Static** | 24-hour diner chain. Chrome counters, bottomless coffee, always half-empty at 3am | **The Pen's booth is at the back of the Harbour branch.** The natural set for late-night hip-hop and R&B scenes |

### The chains everyone actually uses

| Name | Category | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Halo Coffee** | Coffee, ubiquitous | The default "somewhere to buy coffee". Green cups. Everyone has an opinion about it |
| **Tempo Burger** | Fast food, planet-wide | The one every child grows up on. Sponsors the Premier Ongaku League |
| **Sugar Hill Chicken** | Fried chicken | Southside origin, went national, the neighbourhood is bitter about it |
| **Cornerstone Pizza** | Pizza, delivery | Late-night student food. University District institution |
| **Bao Down** | Fast casual, steamed buns | Where the Central District has lunch |
| **Nyala Grill** | East African, family-run | Rose City. Not a chain and proud of it |
| **The Wire & Barrel** | Pub chain | Where ODF servicemen drink |
| **Verrado Bianco** | Expensive Italian | Where Diamonds house does business in daylight |

## Part 3 — Cars

The Mafia have fancy cars, so the marques need to exist. These also give the racing and EDM mixes
their vocabulary.

| Marque | Position | Real-world feel |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Kestrel Motors** | Luxury and armour. The boss car | Rolls-Royce / Bentley |
| **Verrado** | Exotic performance, Italian-coded | Ferrari / Lamborghini |
| **Wolfe & Sons** | Muscle, loud, domestic | Dodge / Ford muscle |
| **Sakata** | Japanese performance, the tuner base | Nissan / Toyota |
| **Bantam** | Everyday, sensible, invisible | Toyota / VW — *what you drive when you do not want to be seen* |
| **NexaGen Motion** | Tech-forward, semi-autonomous | Tesla, but a division of the conglomerate |

> **Bantam is the most useful marque in the list.** "He drives a Bantam" instantly tells you a
> character is either broke, humble, or a professional who understands that the worst car to be
> arrested in is a memorable one.

## Part 4 — Guns

They mostly use guns, so the manufacturers should be named rather than generic.

| Maker | Position |
| --- | --- |
| **Corvid Arms** | Pistols. The street standard. "A Corvid" means a handgun |
| **Ironwood Defense** | Shotguns and rifles. Rural, old, reliable |
| **NexaGen Defence** | Military contracts, smart weapons. What the ODF carries — and what the Mafia cannot legally get |

## Part 5 — Sport

| Institution | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Premier Ongaku League** | Top-flight football. Broadcast by Tower Sports. The thing the whole planet argues about |
| **Ongaku Grand Prix** | Motorsport championship. Legitimate racing, in permanent tension with Chrome's illegal circuit |
| **The Ongaku Cup** | Knockout football competition |
| **Sable Arena** | The capital's biggest indoor venue. Boxing, concerts, championship nights |
| **The Terraces Derby** | The fixture that divides the capital. Southside club vs the Heights club |

**Story value:** a footballer is one of the easiest ordinary-life characters to write — famous,
wealthy, working-class origin, surrounded by people who want something. A Premier Ongaku League
striker who grew up on Freq Kid's block is three sentences of setup and a whole R&B mix.

## Part 8 — Media & press

All owned by **The Tower Group** unless noted (world bible §9).

| Outlet | Category |
| --- | --- |
| **24 Radio** | Music/urban radio — carries the mixes. Overnight show: ***The 24 Hour*** |
| **OBC News** | Television news. The official version of events |
| **The Ongaku Standard** | Newspaper of record |
| **Frequency** | Music magazine. A cover means you have arrived |
| **Static** | Celebrity gossip title. Tower Print's most profitable and most hated product |
| **The Southside Echo** | **Independent** local paper. The only outlet that reports the neighbourhood honestly, permanently near bankruptcy |

> **The Southside Echo is worth more than it looks.** An independent paper with no money, in the
> Mafia's home district, whose reporters know everyone — that is a story generator, and it gives
> Gideon Falk somewhere to go when Tower spikes his piece.

## Part 9 — How to use this

1. **Name it once, keep it forever.** A café named in one mix should reappear three mixes later.
2. **Put institutions in the background of every scene.** A Halo Coffee cup, a Tempo Burger bag, an
   MTA night bus — these cost nothing and do enormous work.
3. **Cross the layers deliberately.** The most valuable connections are the boring ones: a pilot who
   races with a mechanic, a NexaGen analyst dating the wrong person, an enforcer whose daughters go
   to school with a journalist's kids.
4. **Fill in the civilian column before writing anyone's death.** The scene only lands if we know
   what they did on Sundays.
